- 1 AG 586; in 10:35 the sons of Zebedee are making a request: “Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we (ημιν) may ask”. Instead of that, 586 has: “Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever ye (υμιν) may ask”—manifest nonsense. The two letters received the same pronunciation, so someone hearing the text read would understand the first person without question. Even someone reading the text would perceive the obvious error and correct the text in his mind. Since 586 has only this one variant for the whole book, one letter, it is virtually perfect.

> 2 In 2:40, instead of επ αυτω, it has επ αυτο (there is a split in the family at this point). The preposition επι works with both the dative and the accusative cases, and the translation will be the same: “The grace of God was upon him”. If these are two ways of saying the same thing, then 2382 is complete; if not, it is off by one letter for the whole book!

- `3` In 1:11, instead of ουτος Ιησους, it has ουτος ο Ιησους. A demonstrative pronoun defines, even more than a definite article, so the article is redundant here; so they are two ways of saying the same thing: “this very Jesus”. In 26:29 Paul is defending himself before king Agrippa. Instead of ευξαιμην, ‘I would pray’, it has ευξαμην, ‘I do pray’. The indicative is more direct than the optative, but the difference in meaning is slight. If these are not two ways of saying the same thing, then 35 is off by one letter, for the whole book of Acts!

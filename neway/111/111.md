I am pleased to note that the recent *Text und Textwert* for the Apocalypse (2017) recognizes their Complutensian text as an independent line of transmission, along with the so-called Koine and Andreas texts. Their Complutensian is my Family 35; it corresponds to group **<u>c</u>** below. Their Koine corresponds to groups **<u>a</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>b</u>**, **<u>f</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>g</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>i</u>** below. Their Andreas corresponds to groups **<u>d</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>e</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>h</u>** below—well, that is to say, according to my evaluation. As you can see below, there is a good deal of ‘promiscuity’, the individual groups move around, some more than others. The most difficult case is **<u>h</u>** <u>,</u> that goes with the Koine almost as often as with Andreas.

Based on my analysis of Hoskier, the groups have the following ‘size’: **<u>a</u>** is represented by 65 MSS; **<u>b</u>** by 10; **<u>c</u>** by 33;[^1] **<u>d</u>** by 15; **<u>e</u>** by 31; **<u>f</u>** by 11; **<u>g</u>** by 9; **<u>h</u>** by 13; **<u>i</u>** by 11. (**<u>a</u>** alone is larger than **<u>b</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>f</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>g</u>**, **<u>i</u>** combined.) (**<u>d</u>** is smaller than **<u>e</u>** <u>, but</u> **<u>e</u>** is by far the most fragmented group.)[^2] Since I consider **<u>c</u>** to be the common denominator, I place it first; **<u>a</u>** leads the Koine and **<u>d</u>** the Andreas. Only combinations are listed; each group occurs by itself as well.
